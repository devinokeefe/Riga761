# Sources and earlier work

Written on 9 October 2026.

## The archive file

The telegram is in Warsaw, in the Centralne Archiwum Wojskowe (part of the Wojskowe Biuro Historyczne), among
the records of Oddział II Naczelnego Dowództwa Wojska Polskiego, file I.301.8.663. The file is titled
"Radiotelegramy zaszyfrowane nadesłane ze Stacji RTG." (enciphered radiograms sent in by the radio stations) and
was scanned in two parts of 140 and 166 pages. It is one of the Polish radio-intelligence files that the archive
put online in June 2020. The archive's page from that time is gone, but the Internet Archive has copies:

- part 1: <https://web.archive.org/web/20200703151708/https://wbh.wp.mil.pl/c/pages/atts/2020/6/I_301_8_663_1.pdf>
- part 2: <https://web.archive.org/web/20200706011032/https://wbh.wp.mil.pl/c/pages/atts/2020/6/I_301_8_663_2.pdf>
- the archive's list of files: <https://web.archive.org/web/20200703130937/https://wbh.wp.mil.pl/pl/pages/zdigitalizowane-teczki-polskiego-radiowywiadu-wojskowego-z-1920-roku-wpisanego-na-swiatowa-liste-unesco-pamiec-swiata-2020-06-17-kaf5/>

These are the pages around the telegram in part 1, with the page of the PDF and the leaf number pencilled on the
original:

| page | leaf | what |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | 17 | the back of a blank Polish radiotelegraph form, with "Perekop" pencilled on it twice and nothing else |
| 20 | 18 | a typed Polish list of Soviet OGPU and militia schools, of later date and unrelated |
| 21 | 19 | telegram no. 761 |
| 22 | 20 | a slip with two pencilled names, unrelated to this telegram |

The form on page 19 looks like a divider for telegrams in the cipher "Perekop", but no. 761 is the only one in
the file. A look through every page of both parts confirmed it: all the other telegrams are in groups of
figures. Nine more between Moscow and Riga are marked Победа (part 1, pages 7-10 and 15-18). After them comes
traffic between Stockholm and Moscow marked "Perun", and then traffic between Berlin and Moscow, most of it
marked "Pallada", which fills the rest of part 1 and all of part 2. The dates run from late January to the end
of April 1921.

Only the four pages in the table were read properly. The others got just enough attention to tell letters from
figures and a telegram from a form. None of them looks like a worksheet, a key or a decipherment for no. 761,
and the Polish work-up forms for "Pobieda" and "Pallada" have their key grids left empty.

The archive's inventory (<https://wbh-wyszukiwarka.wp.mil.pl/pl/skany/detale_jednostka/680523/>) gives the file
its title and the year 1921 and nothing more. Three other items in the same record group have not been consulted
and could hold a Polish reading:

- I.301.8.656, "Depesze radiotelegraficzne sowieckie dotyczące rokowań pokojowych i operacji wojskowych" (Soviet
  radio despatches concerning the peace negotiations and military operations), dated December 1920 to January
  1921 and not scanned;
- the files of translated cipher messages of 1920-21, I.301.8.659 and 661, which by their titles are Red Army
  traffic;
- I.301.8.658, more cipher telegrams from the radio stations, 1920-21.

## Earlier work

Marek Grajek surveyed the archive's cipher files in "Documents of Polish-Soviet War of 1919-1920 Codebreaking",
*Proceedings of the 4th International Conference on Historical Cryptology, HistoCrypt 2021* (Linköping Electronic
Conference Proceedings 183), pp. 62-69, <https://doi.org/10.3384/ecp183158>. His section 4.18 describes this
file as Soviet cipher messages, most probably in a diplomatic code or cipher, addressed to the head of the Soviet
delegation at Riga. He identifies no system and prints no reading, and the cipher names Perekop and Pobieda do
not occur in the paper. In section 5 he says of the collection as a whole that the Polish codebreakers seem not
to have "undertaken a serious attack at the Soviet diplomatic codes". Nowik, below, says otherwise for 1920.

John Tiltman's memoir "Experiences 1920-1939" was released by the NSA in 2018:
<https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/4895707/Brigadier-John-H-Tiltman-Experiences-1920-1939.pdf>.
On its first page he describes the Soviet traffic between Moscow and London that he worked on from August 1920.
"All messages were enciphered by simple columnar transposition of Russian plain text", he writes, the Russian
having first been put into Latin letters. He adds that about November 1920 a substitution into pairs of figures
was put in front of the transposition. Telegram no. 761 shows the plain transposition, in Cyrillic, still in use
from Riga in February 1921.

The standard work on Polish radio intelligence against Soviet Russia is Grzegorz Nowik, *Zanim złamano "Enigmę".
Polski radiowywiad podczas wojny z bolszewicką Rosją 1918-1920*, 2 vols (Warsaw, 2004 and 2010). It has not been
read for this project, only searched through Google Books, which shows a few lines around each hit and never a
whole page. The hits show this much:

- Polish codebreakers were reading the Soviet delegation's telegrams at Riga in the autumn of 1920. Lieutenant
  Jerzy Suryn, attached to the Polish delegation, deciphered them there until 25 October (vol. 1, p. 237;
  vol. 2, p. 428), and the Cipher Bureau broke Russian diplomatic ciphers as well as military ones (vol. 2,
  pp. 85 and 117).
- Interception of the delegation's traffic went on after the armistice, and from 22 December 1920 army staffs
  were told only to collect cipher telegrams and send them to the high command (vol. 2, p. 137). That fits the
  title of this file.
- No cipher called Perekop is mentioned. "Pobieda" occurs as a reserve key of the Red Army in August 1920
  (vol. 2, pp. 101 and 143). There is no hit for this file, for Frid, or for a Riga telegram of 1921.
- The books stop at the end of 1920, and the author leaves radio intelligence after that to a later book
  (vol. 2, p. 1280).

Someone with the book in front of them should read the pages on Joffe and on Riga in vol. 2 (571, 1075, 1155,
1169, 1209, 1243, 1254, 1256).

## Looking for the telegram in print

If the telegram had ever been printed from the Soviet side, the likeliest places would be the collections of
documents on Soviet foreign policy and on Soviet-Polish relations.

*Документы внешней политики СССР*, vol. 3 (Moscow, 1959), covers 1 July 1920 to 18 March 1921, the months of the
Riga talks: <http://militera.lib.ru/docs/da/dvp/03/index.html>. Telegram no. 761 is not in it and the name Frid
does not occur. This is also the volume from which the trigram table was counted.

*Документы и материалы по истории советско-польских отношений*, vol. 3: April 1920 to March 1921 (Moscow, 1965),
is in the Russian Historical Society's online library:
<https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/133727-dokumenty-i-materialy-po-istorii-sovetsko-polskih-otnosheniy-t-iii-aprel-1920-g-mart-1921-g>.
None of its 275 documents is a telegram from the Riga delegation of January to March 1921. Volume 4 was not
consulted.

*Советско-польские отношения в 1918-1945 гг.*, vol. 1: 1918-1926 (Moscow, 2017), is in the same library:
<https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/446887-sovetsko-polskie-otnosheniya-v-1918-1945-gg-t-1-1918-1926>.
This one does print telegrams of the Riga delegation, in plain text, from the archive of the Russian Foreign
Ministry. None of those from January to March 1921 is dated 4 or 5 February, and Frid, Semashko and the
Commissariat of Health do not come up in them. Their headings and numbers do match the form of telegram no. 761
(see [TEXT.md](TEXT.md)). A search of the whole library for Frid together with Joffe, Chicherin or Riga found
nothing to the point.

The telegram is not in any of four public catalogues of unsolved ciphers, which were searched for this file, the
cipher names, Joffe and Chicherin: Daniel Bourdeau's (github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver),
NoAutopilot/cipher-lab, el-descifrador/cabinet-noir and aaymeloglu/unsolved-ciphers. The second of them names
"Polish and Soviet traffic c.1920" as a field it has decided not to work on. Cryptiana's index and list of
unsolved ciphers (cryptiana.web.fc2.com), Klaus Schmeh's blog, the DECODE database (de-crypt.org) and the
HistoCrypt proceedings from 2018 to 2026 have nothing on this file or these telegrams apart from Grajek's paper.

## What is new

Some of this was known already. Grajek had described the file, Nowik had shown that the Poles could read the
delegation's telegrams in the autumn of 1920, Tiltman had recorded that Soviet diplomatic telegrams of 1920 were
enciphered by columnar transposition, and Frid's career before the war was in a printed register.

New here, as far as the searches above show, are a transcription of telegram no. 761, its key, the text with a
translation, and the match between the doctor it names and the Kamchatka medical inspector of the register. The
key also shows that the cipher marked Перекоп was a single columnar transposition on 26 columns.

No published reading of this telegram, or of any other in the file, was found. The Polish Cipher Bureau had been
reading the delegation's telegrams a few months earlier, and a plain transposition was within its reach, so it
may well have read this one in 1921. If so, the reading is not in the file and has not turned up in print. The
Soviet original, and whatever answer it received, should be in the Russian archives. Unpublished work would not
have shown up in a search like this one.
