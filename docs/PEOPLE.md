# The people and places in the telegram

## Georgy Vladimirovich Frid

The telegram gives the doctor's full name, his twenty-three years of practice and two posts, medical inspector
of the Kamchatka region and head of the evacuation point at Trebizond.

The imperial Ministry of the Interior published a register of everyone entitled to practise medicine in Russia,
the *Россійскій медицинскій списокъ*. Frid is in the volumes for 1913, 1914 and 1916, twice in each. In the list
of staff of the provincial medical administrations he is the only officer entered under the Kamchatka region
(p. XI):

> Обл. врач. инсп. (и. д.) Н. С. Георг. Влад. Фридъ | 1871 | 1897 | Л. | 11 iюня 1897 г. | 2 апр. 1912 г.

The columns after the name are year of birth, year of finishing the course, degree, date of entering the service
and date of appointment to the present post. In full, then: acting regional medical inspector, court councillor
(надворный советник), Georgy Vladimirovich Frid, born 1871, qualified as a physician (лекарь) in 1897, in
government service since 11 June 1897 and in this post since 2 April 1912. He is also in the alphabetical list
of doctors (1914, p. 494):

> Фридъ Георг. Влад. 71 Л. 97. н. с. Врач. инсп. Петропавловскъ, Камчат.

Set beside the telegram:

| the telegram says | the register says |
|---|---|
| Георгий Владимирович Фрид | Георг. Влад. Фридъ |
| formerly medical inspector of the Kamchatka region | acting medical inspector of the Kamchatka region, at Petropavlovsk, from 2 April 1912 |
| twenty-three years of practice (February 1921) | qualified 1897, in service from 11 June 1897: 23 years and 7 months before the telegram |
| head of the Trebizond evacuation point during the war | still at Petropavlovsk on 1 May 1916, the date of the last volume |

The register cannot confirm Trebizond, because its last volume gives the position on 1 May 1916 and Frid was
then still at Petropavlovsk. The dates do leave room for it. The Russian army took Trebizond in April 1916 and
held it until early 1918, so he would have gone there some time after May 1916.

The same volumes list two other doctors named Frid, and neither can be him. They are David Shoilevich, in
private practice at Łódź, and Uriy Khaimovich, a zemstvo doctor in Tambov province.

An earlier volume places him in Riga. This is his entry in the list for 1908:

> Фридъ Георг. Влад. 71 Д. 97. н. с. Орд. б-цы для умалиш. на Александровой Высотѣ. Рига.

This is the same man, born 1871, qualified 1897, a court councillor, working as a staff doctor (ординатор) at
the asylum at Alexandershöhe in Riga. So he had worked in the town before he went east. That he went back to it
after the war, and was on the spot when the delegation needed a doctor, is only a guess.

### How the entries were read

The volumes for 1913, 1914 and 1916 have been scanned by the Russian State Library
(<https://viewer.rsl.ru/ru/rsl01006516585>, <https://viewer.rsl.ru/ru/rsl01006516586> and
<https://viewer.rsl.ru/ru/rsl01006516589>; the entries are on scans 17 and 497, 17 and 514, 17 and 535). The
library's viewer would not load the page images in full, so the entries were read in the OCR text that it gives
for each scan. That text has misreadings in it, such as Фрцдъ for Фридъ in 1914. The 1914 entries were also read
in the transcription at <http://personalhistory.ru/papers/rms1914.htm>.

The 1908 entry comes from a search inside a Google Books reprint of that volume (p. 423), which showed these
lines but not the page. It gives his degree as Д., doctor of medicine, where the later volumes have Л., and that
is probably a misreading.

Nothing was found on the Trebizond evacuation point or who ran it, on Frid in Riga in 1920-21, on Moscow's
answer, or on what became of him.

## The others

- Adolf Joffe (Иоффе), who signs the telegram, chaired the Russian-Ukrainian delegation at the peace talks with
  Poland. The talks moved to Riga in September 1920. The preliminary peace was signed there on 12 October 1920
  and the treaty on 18 March 1921, so the telegram falls between the two.
- Georgy Chicherin, to whom it is addressed, was People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs.
- Nikolai Semashko, for whom it is meant, was People's Commissar of Health from 1918 to 1930. Наркомздрав is his
  commissariat.
- Kamchatka became a region (область) of its own in 1909, with its seat at Petropavlovsk.
- Perekop, the name on the telegram's cipher, is the isthmus that joins the Crimea to the mainland. The Red Army
  forced it in November 1920, three months before the telegram, and drove Wrangel's army out of the Crimea.
